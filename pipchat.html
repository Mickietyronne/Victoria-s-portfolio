01

Overview

Money talk happens in chat. The money doesn’t.

In Nigeria, a lot of everyday payments start in a chat: splitting a bill, sending a gift, paying a vendor. But the payment happens somewhere else. People leave the chat, open a bank app, copy an account number and send a screenshot as proof. Pipchat puts a wallet, Pippay, inside the chat — so the conversation and the money live in the same place.

Sending a ₦5,000 gift today takes five steps

  1. 01 Agree in chat “Abeg send it, I’ll share my details.” ✕ Context lost
  2. 02 Leave the app Switch to a bank app and log in again. ✕ Two apps, two logins
  3. 03 Copy the details Type a 10-digit account number and hope it’s right. ✕ Easy to mistype
  4. 04 Prove it Screenshot the receipt. ✕ No shared record
  5. 05 Back to chat Paste the screenshot and wait. ✕ Trust by screenshot

02

Challenge

As easy as a message, as safe as a bank.

A wallet inside a chat app has to feel casual without being careless. Three constraints shaped almost every screen.

Rules
Identity in tiers

Limits depend on verification — BVN, NIN, selfie, address. Each step has to explain itself or people drop off.

Trust
Fees in plain sight

Every fee is shown before money moves, so nobody meets it for the first time on a statement.

Habit
Chat-speed expectations

People live in WhatsApp. Money features had to feel as quick and familiar as sending a message, or they’d go unused.

04

Inside the app

Six areas of the Pipchat app, and the thinking behind each.

01 · Chat and pay

Payments that live in the conversation.

Money is part of the chat, not a separate screen. A payment shows up as a card in the thread, so it’s the proof and the receipt at once — and groups can run polls and events without leaving the conversation.

02 · Moments and Piptoon

A camera and a character that feel local.

Moments is the story camera: photo, video or text, with filters and a caption. Piptoon gives Pipchat a face: 47 stickers people can save and send in any chat.

03 · Activation and identity

A wallet that explains its own rules.

Nigerian limits depend on how much you verify, so the wallet tells you what each step unlocks. Tier 1 is setup, Tier 2 unlocks an account number, Tier 3 raises limits — and fees appear before money moves.

04 · Virtual cards

Two cards, two money models.

The naira card has its own balance you fund from Pippay. The dollar card has no balance: it pays abroad and charges your wallet in naira at the rate shown when you pay. The screens make that difference obvious.

05 · When things go wrong

Designing the failures too.

27 error states, from a wrong code to a blurry ID. Each one appears inside the screen where it happens, says what went wrong in plain words, and offers a next step.

06 · Help and growth

Support that starts in the app.

Help sits one tap away, with answers by topic before anyone opens a ticket. Referrals reward each friend who joins, and problems can be reported without leaving the app.

06

Decisions

Four calls that made it trustworthy.

01
Activation that explains itself

Tier 1 is setup only, Tier 2 unlocks an account number, Tier 3 raises limits. Every step says what it needs and what it unlocks.

02
Fees before money moves

The 1.8% receiving fee sits on the account card, the top-up screen and the receipt — not hidden in terms.

03
Design the failures too

27 error states, from a wrong OTP to a blurry ID, each shown inside the screen where it happens, with a clear next step.

04
Familiar, but ours

Patterns people already know from WhatsApp, with a local voice: Naija Talk stickers, Piptoon, and copy written for Nigerian users, not a bank.
